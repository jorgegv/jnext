    3.4 Audio¶

    A ZX Spectrum Next makes sound from four kinds of source, summed into one stereo pair: three AY-3-8910-compatible sound chips, four 8-bit sample DACs, the original Spectrum's one-bit beeper, and a digital audio input from the Raspberry Pi interface. jnext models all four and sums them exactly as the FPGA's mixer does — and then has to do something the hardware never has to do, which is hand a stream of samples to a host sound card whose clock is not the emulated machine's clock.

    That last step is why audio splits in two, and why keeping the halves apart matters more here than anywhere else in jnext. Everything in src/audio/ up to and including the 13-bit sum models the FPGA's audio path and is checkable against ym2149.vhd, soundrive.vhd and audio_mixer.vhd. Everything after it — the ring buffer, the SDL device, and above all the pacing — is host policy with no VHDL counterpart. A change on the first side is a hardware-fidelity question; a change on the second is a "does it sound right on this machine" question, and the two are settled by completely different kinds of evidence.

    Nothing in the emulated half knows the host exists. The sources are advanced from the instruction loop (see A frame, end to end) and the mixer writes into a ring buffer; if no one ever drains it, the machine runs on exactly as before, silently. That is what makes --headless and --silent cheap rather than special cases.

    The emulated hardware¶

    AY / YM2149 — src/audio/ay_chip.*. The AY-3-8910 is the 128K Spectrum's sound chip, and almost all Spectrum music is written for it: three square-wave tone channels, one noise generator, a shared envelope generator, and sixteen registers reached through a select/data port pair. One AyChip is a full YM2149 — the tone counters, the 17-bit noise LFSR, the envelope generator with its sixteen shapes, and both the AY and YM volume tables taken from the VHDL. It is advanced by tick() at the PSG clock-enable rate, 28 MHz / 16 = 1.75 MHz, and exposes three 0-255 channel levels.

    TurboSound — TurboSound is the Next's name for carrying three of those chips at once, all sharing the same pair of ports: writing a control value to port 0xFFFD chooses which chip subsequent register writes reach, so a program has nine tone channels available where a 128K has three, plus per-chip stereo placement. src/audio/turbosound.* owns the three chips and everything around them — the chip selection, per-chip panning, the ABC/ACB stereo mode (NR 0x08 b5), the per-chip mono flags (NR 0x09 b7:5), the TurboSound enable (NR 0x08 b1) and AY-vs-YM mode (NR 0x06 b0). All three chips advance on every PSG tick, whether or not TurboSound is on or the chip is selected, as in the VHDL (every ym2149 runs on the ungated clock_en_i). The enable and the selection decide only which chips reach the mix: with TurboSound off, the selected one. The latched 12-bit stereo pair is recomputed only when a chip output or a mix input changed, and the mixer only ever reads that latched pair.

    There are four entry points, and the differences are load-bearing. power_on() (construction and a hard reset, which on hardware reconfigures the FPGA) puts every chip at its declared initial values. reset() is the VHDL reset pulse, which is what a soft reset is: RESET_H on the chips clears the address, the register file (R7 = 0xFF) and the outputs, and nothing else, so the tone, noise and envelope generators and the divider keep running; it also clears the NR-driven enable, stereo and mono inputs, which Emulator::init re-derives from the NextReg shadows. reset_ay_only() is the same RESET_H effect without touching those NR-driven inputs, because in hardware they are external signals into the chip rather than state inside it. set_reset_held() is the NR 0x06 psg_mode = 11 level (audio_ay_reset is a level in the VHDL, not a pulse): while it is set, port writes and the select/pan command are ignored and the outputs stay 0, but the generators keep running. The emulator applies reset_ay_only() when the hold starts and re-derives the level from NR 0x06 after init() and after a state load; it is not serialised. The PSG clock-enable phase (psg_accum_) is a free-running divider and also survives a soft reset. At power-on the PSG mode is YM, and init() derives AY or YM from the NR 0x06 value like the other NR 0x06 shadows.

    DAC — src/audio/dac.*. Where an AY synthesises a waveform, a DAC simply plays back: write a byte, get a voltage, and the program is responsible for feeding it fast enough. Several third-party Spectrum peripherals did exactly that — Specdrum, Soundrive, Covox — each answering on its own port addresses, and the Next decodes all of them onto the same four 8-bit channels. A+B sum to left, C+D to right, giving a 9-bit value per side; each channel resets to 0x80, which is its silence, not zero.

    The port decode does not live in dac.*. It is in Emulator::init in src/core/emulator.cpp, because each port is gated by NR 0x84 bits exactly as the VHDL decodes them:

    Ports Channels Gate (NR 0x84)
    0x1F, 0x0F, 0x4F, 0x5F A, B, C, D (Soundrive mode 1) b1
    0xF1, 0xF3, 0xF9, 0xFB A, B, C, D (Soundrive mode 2) b2
    0x3F, 0x5F A, D (Profi Covox) b3
    0x0F, 0x4F B, C (Covox) b4
    0xFB A and D (Pentagon/ATM, mono) b5, effective only while b2 is off (zxnext.vhd:2749)
    0xB3 B and C (GS Covox) b6
    0xDF A and D (Specdrum, mono) b7
    NR 0x2C / 0x2D / 0x2E B / A+D / C —

    Every write is additionally gated on NR 0x08 b3; with the DAC disabled the Soundrive module is held in reset. The family decode comes first: a port whose NR 0x84 family bit is off is not a DAC port at all, whatever NR 0x08 says. The mono ports writing two channels is real hardware behaviour and a routine source of confusion — a program that expects 0xDF to be "channel D" gets A as well.

    Dropped writes. Every audio write the machine discards is counted by the reason the VHDL gives for discarding it, so a debugger can say why a program is silent. AudioDropReason (src/debug/events.h) has eight reasons, and a write gets the first that applies: decode before gate, because an undecoded write is not a write to that device. The AY port gate (NR 0x84 b0) comes before the reset hold (NR 0x06 psg_mode = 11), which comes before the 0xFFFD value and the register address. The decision stays where it is made: TurboSound::reg_addr() and reg_write() return an AyPortStatus, and AyChip::write_data() returns false for an address of 16-31; Emulator::note_ay_status_() maps the status to a reason, and each DAC handler's gate goes through Emulator::dac_gate_(), family first, then the DAC enable. The 0xF1 and 0xF9 handlers count a closed Soundrive 2 family and then decline, so the byte still reaches the paging decode (zxnext.vhd:3024); the 0xFB handler counts its neither-decode case. The 0xDFFD write is not counted: its handler wins dispatch while NR 0x82 b2 is set, so the AY side never sees it. Emulator::note_audio_drop_() records the count and the last port, value, PC, frame tag and source; the source is Debugger outside guest execution, else the live write-source bracket the DMA and the Copper set around their port writes. The counters are host state: outside describe_state(), init() and load_state(), carried over a cold boot by emulator_cold_boot() beside the mute mask, cleared only by Debugger::clear_audio_drops(), and not counted while a rewind replays frames. Debugger::audio_state() publishes them as AudioState::drops.

    The write tap. Every decoded audio write can also be logged, for the debugger's Log tab and --audio-log. Emulator holds one AudioTapSink* (src/debug/audio_tap.h, the core-to-backend seam, implemented by the debugger backend) and one bool, audio_tap_active_; the backend switches it on while any client asks for the log or a live AudioWrite subscription exists, or a .psg recording runs (Emulator::set_audio_tap(), called by publish_audio_tap()). The sites are the three AY handlers (0xFFFD, 0xBFFD and 0xBFF5), the 14 DAC channel writes in the 11 DAC handlers (Emulator::dac_write_() wraps each), the three NextREG 0x2C-0x2E mirror handlers, the 0xFE handler and note_audio_drop_(), so the counter and the log come from one call. Each site tests the one bool and nothing else, and there is no test on the per-instruction path. Rule R1: the tap observes and never writes. The sites differ in order: the AY handlers (0xFFFD, 0xBFFD, 0xBFF5) read what the entry needs (the chip, the address, the previous byte) before the write and record after it, while the DAC writes (dac_write_()), the NR 0x2C-0x2E mirrors and the 0xFE handler record BEFORE it (the previous value is still in place, and tap_beeper_ must run before beeper_.set_ear()). Either way the hot path's own writes stay where they are, and the log cannot drift from them. The sink has three consumers, the log ring, the AudioWrite event latch (09-2) and the .psg recorder (below), and the core knows of none. The cold bodies are in src/core/emulator_audio_tap.cpp, marked cold and out of line, so adding one cannot move inlining in emulator.cpp. They return at once while a rewind replays frames (replay_mode_). A plain register select is not logged; an applied 0xFFFD write with bit 7 set is exactly the chip-select or pan command, because a bit-7 value that is not the command returns SelectIgnored. An entry's timestamp comes from Emulator::stamp_at_(), the arithmetic the event latch stamper also uses, so the log agrees with the event system by construction; the stamp is the start of the instruction slot that raised the write, which is also why a rewind landing removes the entries at its own cycle. The landing removes everything from that cycle on, markers included: the rewind ring is this session's own history on one clock, and a soft reset, a rewind and a state load taken from it all keep that clock (loading a checkpoint of this session puts it back to the checkpoint's cycle), so every marker is crossed. A state-load marker AT the landing cycle is the landing state itself and stays, just before the new Rewind marker. A state loaded from another session has a clock that cannot be ordered against this one, so across such a load the cycle order, and the result, is best-effort. The Rewind marker is always recorded, and only the same marker twice at one cycle is one row, so a reset right after a rewind keeps its own row. Markers come from debug_after_machine_transition_() (every reset and every state load pass through it; a ring restore for a rewind is left out by DebugState::restoring_from_rewind()) and from the two rewind landings. A hard reset rebuilds the Emulator, so the backend installs the sink again in reapply_after_machine_rebuild(). The ring, the seq counter and the verbs live in the backend (src/debug/debugger_audio.cpp).

    Pacing engines. Debugger::ctc_state() and Debugger::dma_state() (INS-25) report what paces a sample player: the CTC's four channels and the DMA's prescaler. Both read through a DebugView that is defined inline in CtcChannel and Dma (ctc.h, dma.h): no data member, so no layout change, and no core translation unit calls it, so no code is emitted in a hot path; an unused debugger pays nothing. The CTC rate is the ZC/TO rate: MASTER_CLOCK_HZ / (prescaler x TC) for a running timer (TC 0 counts 256; ctc_chan.vhd:132-170), the source's rate divided by TC for a running counter. A counter's source is the previous channel, channel 0 being fed by channel 3 (zxnext.vhd:4598, Ctc::handle_zc_to arms (channel + 1) & 3); Debugger::ctc_state() walks that chain back at most four hops, a stopped channel or a ring of counters ends it with no rate, and the division is rounded once, at the end, in milli-hertz. A channel is running only in S_RUN or S_RUN_TC, the states in which t_count counts. The DMA rate is the NOMINAL pacing rate MASTER_CLOCK_HZ / (32 x prescaler), the same at every CPU speed (the DMA timer adds 8, 4, 2 or 1 per CPU clock at 3.5, 7, 14 and 28 MHz, one per master cycle, dma.vhd:250-255): the gate (dma.vhd:424) is a minimum spacing from the read, and the state machine's own cycles come on top, so a real stream is slower than the nominal rate.

    The sample stream. dac_stream_stats() (src/debug/dac_stream.h, a published header with no core dependency) turns the audio log into one row per DAC writer: the measured rate, the interval minimum, mean, maximum and lower median, the population standard deviation and the gaps. Its rules are small and each is pinned by a row. An entry counts if it is an applied DAC or mirror write after the newest marker of the vector (a reset restarts the player, a rewind truncated the log) inside the window (end - window, end]; a clock younger than the window has no start. The log has one entry per DAC channel, so a write to 0xDF, 0xB3, the mono 0xFB or NextREG 0x2D is two entries at one cycle: consecutive counted entries with equal cycle, source, PC and port are ONE sample. A CPU writer is keyed by its PC; DMA, Copper and debugger entries by source alone, because their PC is the slot's, not theirs. A gap is an interval strictly above 1.5 times the lower median: one skipped sample is two periods, one period is one, so 1.5 sits between them whatever the interrupt acceptance jitter does to a single interval. nearest_pacing() pairs a CPU writer with the running CTC channel whose rate is nearest and a DMA writer with the DMA. The DMA's own spacing is at least the prescaler's (dma.vhd, the timer cleared at TRANSFERING_READ_1 and the gate at WAITING_CYCLES), so its measured rate is at most the nominal one.

    The .psg recorder. Debugger::psg_record_start() / psg_record_stop() (INS-24) and --psg-record record each chip's register stream in the layout of Fuse 1.6.0's AY log (psg.c: psg_start_recording, psg_frame, write_frame_separator, psg_stop_recording; the source tree is in doc/REFERENCES.md). Two classes do it. audio::PsgWriter (src/audio/psg_writer.h) is the pure encoder: begin(), frame(), end(), with the frame numbers the caller's. It knows the header, the register order (R0-R13 ascending, R14/R15 never) and the separators: a block at frame F follows the previous block's frame L by F - L separator frames, and the end by S + 1 - L, where a run of n frames is FE k for every 4k <= n (k <= 255) and then one FF each. dbg::PsgRecorder (src/debug/psg_recorder.h, internal like audio_tap.h) is the third consumer of the tap: Debugger::Impl::AudioLogSink feeds it every entry, markers included, before the ring test, so a recording needs no log and loses nothing to the ring's size, a Log Clear or the marker dedup. It groups with one AudioFrameAccumulator for the three chips and writes each completed frame to the chip's file: FILE for AY#0, -ay1 / -ay2 before the extension for the others, created at that chip's first block with their writer begun at the recording's start frame so the three stay aligned. The tap is on while psg is set (publish_audio_tap()), and sync_audio_log(force) emits the rebuild's Reset marker when a recording runs as well as when the log is asked for.

    Frame mapping. A file's frames never go back and never repeat, though the stamper's frame number does (a hard reset restarts it, a rewind and a state load put it back). file frame = stamper frame + off; seen is the highest file frame of any non-marker entry (DAC, beeper and select entries too). A discontinuity is a marker, or a frame number below the last entry's. At one, off = max(off, seen + 1 - M) with M the new frame number, except a StateLoad, which always takes seen + 1 - M because a loaded frame number says nothing about time that played. So a soft reset, or any marker whose numbering still moves forward, keeps the numbering and a reset in the frame of the last write splits it, and anything that would go back continues right after the last frame seen. The frame the accumulator returns at a discontinuity is written with the mapping in force before it. The writes of a rewound future were played and stay in the file. A hard reset or a state load keeps elapsed time: Impl::prepare_rebuild() (every rebuild path: reset_hard(), load() and the guest's on_cold_boot_begin()) and Debugger::load_state_bytes() call PsgRecorder::note_time(frame_tag(emu)) before the machine is replaced, which raises seen to the frame current at that moment, so the new frame 0 (or the loaded frame number, which means nothing) maps right after it. Menu and Quick Load loads reach a rebuild through cold_boot() and so prepare_rebuild(). A chip file that cannot be created is tried once (chip_failed_): it flags the I/O error stop() reports and that chip's blocks are dropped. The destructor finishes the files up to seen, which main()'s early returns after --psg-record started rely on.

    Differences from Fuse, both kept out of the fixture. psg_write_register() keeps the FIRST value written to a register in a frame and jnext keeps the last (the tap's value is the raw byte the guest wrote, as Fuse's is). Fuse keeps 4 bits of a register select (set_current_register: value & 0x0f), so a select of 0x1D writes R13 there; the Next drops the data write (AyAddressHigh, counted as dropped).

    The check against Fuse. test/audio/fixtures/psg_fixture.asm is a program of our own: a gate that waits for a key, a sentinel frame writing R7 = 0x3F, 8 empty frames, then 300 frames of pseudo-random register sets written in descending order, with gaps of 1, 3, 4, 5, 9 and 1100 frames (the 1100 checks the clamp of a separator run at 255) and an R14-only frame, an R15 and R0 frame. Writes come about 20000 T-states after the HALT, so the frame they fall in does not depend on where an emulator puts its interrupt. psg_fixture.fuse.psg is Fuse's recording of it, made by tools/fuse-psg-reference.sh (it needs fuse, xdotool and xvfb-run, drives File > AY Logging > Record... on an Xvfb display, and is never run by CI). Both the reference and jnext's recording are cut to the body from the sentinel on (psg_normalise() in audio_debug_test.cpp, tools/psg-normalise.py for the shell; both pinned to the same body length) and compared byte for byte: in process on a 128K machine (PSF-01..03) and through --psg-record in the psg-record-func regression row. To regenerate the reference after changing the program, run the tool, check the new body length and update the two pins: PSG_FUSE_BODY_LEN in audio_debug_test.cpp and n_fuse in test/00regression/scripts/psg-record-func.sh. A program change that moves where a write falls relative to the frame interrupt is the one thing that can change the body without changing the program's intent, so the reference is recorded again whenever psg_fixture.bin is.

    What the taps cost. Every consumer sits behind one test that is false when nobody asks, and the rule is measured, not assumed: on build/gui-release-non-pgo, test/bench/perf-attach.sh (retired instructions, user mode, three interleaved reps, medians) over the eight bench.sh workloads, a tree with the taps built in and off stays within 0.1 % of one without them (the largest difference is a constant start-up cost of about 70 to 140 thousand instructions, the size of the extra translation units). With a tap on, the cost depends on what the program does, not on the tap alone. The write log is per row: 7200 instructions per logged row on a DAC player writing 650 times a frame (+15.6 % over the run), nothing on a program that rarely writes. The source tap (Scopes) and the stems turn the HALT and raster-poll batches off while they run, so the cost is the unbatched path's: +7.7 % over a boot to the 48K ROM, +115 % on an idle HALT loop at 3.5 MHz, about 5 to 8 times the instructions on the batched demo programs and on the same loop at 28 MHz (Scopes), and +21.5 % on a boot and +525 % on a DAC player at 28 MHz (stems). The user guide gives a user the host-CPU figure for Scopes. A new tap or consumer repeats this A/B before it lands, and a difference over 0.1 % is bisected to its cause instead of accepted.

    Frame grouping and the tracker. src/debug/audio_frames.h is a pure, published helper that groups the log's entries, oldest first, into one AudioFrame per frame that holds counted AY writes (AudioFrameAccumulator::feed()). A counted write is an applied AyRegister entry (no Dropped flag, chip 0-2, register 0-15); any other non-marker entry only proves the open frame ended. A frame keeps the last value written to each of R0-R13, the value at its start (before: the prev of the frame's first write to the register, which is exact because the AY stores the whole written byte; for a register not written in the frame, the value carried from the previous frame, or unknown after a marker), a mask of the registers written and of those written twice, the first and last raster stamps and the PC range of the CPU's writes, and the number of empty frames before it. A marker, or a frame number that goes backwards, completes the open frame and forgets the timeline. The Audio panel's Tracker (src/debugger/audio_tracker_view.*) is its consumer. The Tracker mirrors the log by the Log view's own rule, one function (fetch_audio_log(): append the entries after the newest one held, or resync when that entry is gone) and groups only what is new, keeping at most kMaxRows rows. Nothing in the emulation path changes for it: it reads what the write tap already records.

    The player timing strip under the Tracker's table is the header's second consumer. audio_timing_stats() takes completed frames, oldest first, and a window of frame numbers ending at the newest, and returns min / avg / max of the first and last write's line and T-state and of the write count, the union of the CPU PC ranges, and the frame numbers with no write. It is total on any input: a frame number that does not go up starts a new run and only the frames after the last break count, and the frame numbers before the oldest frame used, minus its empty_before, are not counted as empty because the log says nothing about them (its ring, a marker, the row cap). The view feeds it AudioTrackerModel::timing_frames(), the model's own rows: the open frame's tail is left out, a reset or state load row ends the walk and a rewind row does not. The strip is recomputed by AudioTrackerView::update_timing() at the end of refresh(), so it costs nothing while another sub-tab is current, and again when the window box or the current row changes. The window box is a view control with no setting. The dot plot, AudioJitterPlot, scales Y to the window's own lines.

    Beeper — src/audio/beeper.*. The beeper is the 48K Spectrum's entire sound system: a single bit in port 0xFE that the CPU toggles, with everything from clicks to three-channel music produced by how fast it toggles. jnext's model is deliberately tiny — three booleans, being EAR and MIC from port 0xFE bits 4 and 3 plus a tape-EAR input from real-time tape playback, and one derived value, mic_final, the board's beep_mic_final (zxnext.vhd:6503), which is what the mixer's MIC term reads. mic_final is not in the saved state: the emulator refreshes it from Emulator::tape_out_level at the events that change it. There is no accumulator and no filtering here, because that is the mixer's job.

    Pi I2S — src/audio/i2s.*. A Next can take digital audio in from a Raspberry Pi attached to its interface, and mix it with everything else. jnext models the destination, not the wire: a latched 10-bit sample pair plus the NR 0xA2 control byte, with no I2S protocol emulation. Its idle value (0x200, offset binary) is load-bearing. The samples come from NextPi: QEMU's wav audio back-end writes the emulated Pi's sound into a FIFO (a named pipe on Windows, pi_audio_win.cpp), src/audio/pi_audio.* reads it on a thread into a ring, and the emulator latches one frame per mixer output sample into I2s (Emulator::feed_pi_audio), or 0x200 when none is ready. PiAudio absorbs the drift between QEMU's host clock and the emulated one: it prebuffers 50 ms, plays silence on an underrun and prebuffers again, and trims a backlog past 300 ms back to 100 ms. NR 0xA2 bit 0 sends the Pi's audio to EAR instead: I2s::fe_ear() is the hardware's comparator with hysteresis (zxnext.vhd:2677-2690), and Emulator::audio_ear_in folds it into the port 0xFE bit 6 read and the MIC output through the board's ear_relax. While it does (I2s::ear_routed), PiAudio runs in tape mode: a 250 ms prebuffer, and drift taken out one frame at a time, because a tape loader cannot survive the refill's gap or a trim. The chapter on peripherals describes how NextPi itself runs.

    The mixer¶

    src/audio/mixer.* reproduces audio_mixer.vhd's 13-bit unsigned sum exactly: EAR contributes 512, MIC 128, the AY pair is zero-extended, the DAC pair is shifted left by two, and the I2S pair is zero-extended. Mixer::mix is a pure function of the sources, holding no state of its own.

    The hardware mixer has no tape input. A tape playing in real time, and the Pi's audio when NR 0xA2 sends it to EAR, reach the mix only inside the MIC term: beep_mic_final is the board's EAR input XOR the port 0xFE MIC bit (on the issue 3 keyboard setting), so a tape swings the mix by 128, a quarter of a beeper EAR step, and a tape XOR a set MIC bit is heard inverted. The EAR input itself is the jack through the board's ear_relax: a level held with no edge for 64 x 512 master cycles relaxes to the issue 2 MIC level. jnext applies that to both sources of the XOR: the Pi's comparator edges and the playing tape's own level changes (tape_edge_master_, stamped where the tape block sees the level move). A tape pause holds its last level for a second, so the relaxation is what a loader sees after the last pulse of a block. The stamp is not saved: after a restore (load_state, a .jns deck reattach) derive_tape_edge_ rebuilds it from the deck's position (a .jns load calls it again after the deck is reattached, as load_state runs before that). TapLoader::ticks_since_edge is exact for every phase (the pulse or pause length minus pulse_left) except the very first pilot pulse of a tape started from idle, which has no edge, so a restore inside it is approximate (it matters only on issue 2 with MIC set); WavLoader::ticks_since_edge looks back along the recording (to twice the hold, all the relaxation needs); TzxLoader::ticks_since_edge is exact in the pause (the 1 ms hold and the pause's remainder, lengths the player holds) and 0 in a pulse phase, because the ZOT player does not keep the length of the pulse it has just started: the level is then taken as freshly changed, which keeps it as the board has it until the relaxation time, at most one pulse late for a pulse longer than that. Emulator::tape_out_level is the one model of that signal (the MIC jack of a tape save reads the same value), and it is refreshed into the beeper at the events that change it: a port 0xFE write, an NR 0x08 or NR 0xA2 write, a state load, a replay-mode change, and a level change of a playing tape, in the instruction of the tape block that shows it (one compare per instruction while a tape plays, nothing when none does). Emulator::mic_stale_ covers what has no event of its own, at each output-sample boundary: a tape that started or stopped, and the Pi's relaxation hold running out (also inside a HALT batch). A tape edge is exact to the instruction, as EAR is, because the mixer integrates the term across the sample. A state load also re-derives the NR 0x06 shadows (speaker-exclusive gate, NMI button enables, PS/2 mode, hotkey enables) from the restored register.

    Two things around that sum are jnext's own rather than the hardware's, and both arrived as bug fixes:

    • Box filtering, not point sampling. accumulate() integrates the source levels over a span of emulated master cycles and emit_sample() divides by the span. Output samples are roughly 635 master cycles apart while a beeper engine toggles far faster, so point-sampling would discard most toggles and fold their energy back into the audible band as a whistle. Emulator::advance_audio walks each instruction's span in chunks that stop at output-sample boundaries, which makes the weighting exact at instruction granularity.
    • AC coupling. Mixer::MIX_REST_LEVEL is the sum a fully silent machine produces — the DAC midpoints plus the I2S idle level — and emit_sample() subtracts it, so digital silence really is zero, exactly as the hardware's output capacitor blocks DC. Getting this constant wrong does not sound like a DC offset. It makes every buffer seam a step of that height, heard as a click train.

    Output is stereo int16_t at 44 100 Hz into a 4096-pair ring buffer, about four frames' worth, which drops the oldest sample on overrun. Two optional callbacks tap the same samples, one for --record video muxing and one for --wav-record (AudioRecorder). Per-source gains and the debugger's mute mask are host settings rather than machine state, so they are excluded from reset() and from snapshots, and the cold boot of a hard reset carries both across (the gains in the boot config, the mask in emulator_cold_boot()).

    The mask must not reach a snapshot, and the TurboSound sum is state, so TurboSound keeps two sums: the hardware one (every active chip; what a snapshot or a rewind slot stores, under the keys pcm_l and pcm_r) and the audible one (pcm_left(), pcm_right(), chip_pcm()), which leaves out the chips the mask mutes. A load sets the audible pair equal to the loaded hardware pair, and the first PSG tick rebuilds both. A state saved while a chip is muted is therefore byte-identical to one saved unmuted.

    The beeper bit of the mask zeroes the mixer's ear and mic terms, and because a playing tape reaches the mix only through mic_i (above), it silences the tape and the Pi's audio routed to EAR as well; the Audio panel's Tape EAR lamp still shows the raw input (BeeperState::tape_ear), so a muted tape is visibly playing.

    The source tap. The debugger's Scopes view needs the level of each source before the mixer sums them, which the mixer does not keep. AudioSourceTap (src/audio/audio_source_tap.h, standard headers only) holds sixteen accumulators and a 8192-sample ring per source; the Debugger owns it, the Emulator holds a raw pointer (set_audio_scope_tap()), and it is not machine state: not saved, not reset, never read by the Z80. Mixer::integrate_span_tapped() is integrate_span()'s loop, chunk for chunk, with tap_accumulate_() beside the audible accumulate_impl_(): the sixteen terms are the hardware ones (Dac::channel(), AyChip::output_a/b/c(), TurboSound::hw_pcm_left() and the beeper's EAR and mic_final() levels: the playing tape and the Pi's audio routed to EAR have no term of their own, they arrive inside beep_mic_final (v1.3.62), so they show in the Beeper source as its MIC 128), computed beside mix() and never inside it, so no mute and no host gain reaches them. Each pushed sample is the mixer's own rounded_average_x4 of a term over one output period, so the Left and Right traces equal the mixer's output plus its resting level exactly. The sync rule: a tap sample is pushed only when the tap saw the mixer's whole sample (compared by cycle count), which covers a tap enabled mid-sample, a reset, a state load and a switch between the tapped and untapped paths.

    Nothing may be added to the hot audio path when no tap is installed (a debugger feature that is unused costs nothing). A test per instruction slice was rejected, and so was a second per-slot accumulate entry point (a second caller of a hot function stopped the compiler inlining it, 1 to 7 % of every program). Instead the device cluster's replay_mode_ test became a test of one byte, Emulator::audio_divert_ (bit 0 a replay, bit 1 a tap installed): the same load, test and branch, so zero costs nothing extra, and a non-zero byte goes to audio_divert_span_(), which is cold and out of line and calls integrate_span_tapped() (a replay mixes nothing). refresh_audio_divert_() runs at every writer of replay_mode_ and of the tap pointer.

    The HALT and raster-poll batches do not feed the tap: a test in their replay loop cost one instruction per replayed segment (0.1 % of whole programs, measured), and a twin of the replay cost more elsewhere. While a tap is installed batch_window_limit_() refuses every window (it tests audio_divert_, which it already did for a rewind replay as replay_mode_), so those programs run slot by slot, the path the batches are held equivalent to, and the tap sees exactly the batch-off samples. Batching resumes when the tap is dropped.

    The WAV stems. The same tap carries seven more terms while a stem recording runs (AudioStemTap, hung on AudioSourceTap::stems): each AY chip's left and right, the DAC's, the beeper, the Pi's and the mix, in the mixer's 13-bit levels times master cycles, accumulated in tap_accumulate_() only when the pointer is set. They are the hardware terms of audio_mixer.vhd (pcm = ear + mic + ay + dac + i2s, one unsaturated register), so every one is before the debugger mutes and the host gains: TurboSound::hw_chip_pcm(), Dac::channel() (not the audible copy), the beeper's exc_i-gated EAR and mic_final(), pi_audio_L/R() (or its rest level), and the mix. At each output-sample boundary a frame is pushed, one value per stem and channel, each the mixer's own x4 round-to-nearest average less the source's rest level (AY 0, DAC 4096, beeper 0, Pi 2048; the four add up to the mixer's MIX_REST_LEVEL * 4 of 6144, so the AC coupling splits exactly), so the mix stem equals --wav-record at 0 dB. The stems have their own sample counter and the same whole-sample rule as the scope.

    Where the sum is taken, and its bound. The identity is on the hardware 13-bit mix, time-averaged by the mixer's box filter and scaled by four; everything after it (output gain, per-source gains, mutes, the int16 clamp) is host-side and absent from the stems. Nothing in between saturates or filters (the VHDL sum is at most 5998 of 8191, the AY sum never wraps). Each file is one rounding of an exact average x_i = 4 acc_i / cycles, and the mix is one rounding of their sum M. With round(x) = floor(x + 1/2) the error r = x - round(x) is in [-1/2, 1/2), so the sum of the six source files minus the mix file is r_M - (r_1 + ... + r_6), an integer in (-3.5, 3.5): at most 3 counts. It is zero when every 4 acc_i is a multiple of the cycle count, which holds whenever each term is constant over the sample.

    The per-chip latch. A chip's stem must be the psgN_L/R_pan register whose sum the mixer heard (turbosound.vhd:323-335), but TurboSound keeps only the sum, and recomputing a chip's value on the fly disagrees with the latched sum between a pan, select, mono, ACB or enable write and the next PSG tick (up to 16 master cycles; at 28 MHz an OUT is shorter than that). So set_hw_chip_latch(true) makes the cold recompute (compute_stereo_mix_channels_()) store each chip's hardware pair beside the sum. The test that sends the recompute out of line is the existing channel_mute_mask_ != 0: the latch is bit 15 of that mask (the mute bits are 0-8; channel_mute_mask() masks it off), so the hot loop is unchanged. A separate mix_divert_ byte in that test was tried first and measured +0.1 to +0.3 % retired instructions on every workload. Before the first latched recompute (just after the latch is switched on, or after a state load) hw_chip_pcm() computes the pair from the current inputs, which is exact unless an input change is pending, and that can only be for one PSG tick. The latch is not machine state: not serialised, and reset() only re-validates it.

    Alignment. A frame is pushed for every sample the mixer emits, from the first sample that starts after the recording starts. Emulator::init() discards the mixer's partial sample, so it also calls AudioSourceTap::clear_partial(), and so does set_audio_scope_tap() when it installs a tap that is not the one already installed (it also switches the latch to match stems, and a re-install of the same tap, which the backend does after an in-place load, keeps the partial sample): otherwise the next sample would not be whole for one of the two and one stem frame would be lost per reset. A cold boot builds a new Emulator whose tap is installed by the backend's reconstruct re-application, before its first frame. Stem recording installs the tap, so it refuses the HALT and raster-poll batches like a scope does.

    StemRecorder (src/debug/stem_recorder.*, internal to the backend) owns the AudioStemTap and seven AudioRecorders; the Debugger holds it as a recording, not a client request (stems_record_start() and stems_record_stop(), INS-24), so tabs and windows do not end it and --wav-record-stems reaches the same path through app.debugger().

    The envelope generator takes one step right after every R13 write, as the VHDL does: the write pulses env_reset, which reloads the level and also forces env_ena (ym2149.vhd:340-342 and ym2149.vhd:393-409), so the first step comes with the write instead of one envelope period later. AyChip::load_envelope() applies the reload and then runs step_shape_(), the half of update_envelope() that follows the period counter.

    A second host mask, the 13-bit per-channel one (AudioChannelMute, set through Emulator::set_audio_channel_mute_mask()), follows the same rules and composes with the first. Its nine AY bits go to TurboSound, which zeroes the muted channel's input BEFORE the chip's A/B/C mux when it builds the audible pair, so ACB, mono and pan stay exact; the hardware pair never sees them. Its four DAC bits go to the Dac, which keeps an audible copy of its four registers (a muted channel reads its idle 0x80), updated on each write and on a mute change; pcm_left() and pcm_right() sum that copy, so the Mixer's mix() is the same code muted or not, and the registers (what the debugger reads and a snapshot stores) never see the mute. A state load rebuilds the copy. The cold boot carries both masks.

    Getting samples to the host¶

    src/platform/sdl_audio.* is the only place SDL audio appears. The device is opened in callback mode: SDL's audio thread pulls samples from a fixed-size ring buffer owned by SdlAudio, and push_from_mixer() drains the mixer into that ring once per frontend tick. It refuses to push when the ring is already past QUEUE_MAX_MS, and it never clears the ring — discarding samples that are queued or freshly read punches a hole in the stream, and a hole in the stream is a click.

    The callback's shortfall policy is the pure header src/platform/audio_fill.h: whatever the ring cannot cover is filled by repeating the last real sample pair delivered — a DC hold, never zeros — so the device can never play content jnext did not choose, no matter what the GUI thread is doing. Before this, the queue-push model let SDL inject zeros whenever the queue ran dry, which over any nonzero programme content is an audible click train.

    Headless opens no device at all; the mixer still synthesises and the ring buffer simply self-limits. --silent goes further and makes Emulator::tick_devices_after_instruction skip PSG ticking and mixer synthesis entirely. Register writes still land, so the machine state stays correct — only the oscillator work is skipped.

    Pacing — pure host policy¶

    This has no hardware counterpart at all, and it decides whether jnext sounds broken. The mixer emits exactly 44 100 samples per emulated second, but a frontend driven by a 20 ms timer runs 50.00 frames per real second (see A frame, end to end), and no machine's frame is exactly 20 ms. That small permanent mismatch drains the device queue to empty within about twenty seconds, after which every shortfall plays content the emulator never produced — under the original push model, SDL-injected zeros several times a second, forever.

    The fix is to pace on the sound card instead of on the wall clock. src/platform/audio_pacing.h holds the whole policy as pure constexpr functions: frames_for_tick() decides from the device queue depth whether this host tick runs 0, 1 or 2 emulated frames, holding the depth in a 40-90 ms band. It acts on an EMA of the readings rather than raw ones, because the device consumes in roughly 23 ms chunks and that sawtooth would clip the band edges constantly; it feeds each intervention's effect forward so filter lag cannot over-correct; and it keeps two emergency guards on the raw readings, outside the smoothing. The estimate is also kept within one device chunk of each reading: the feed-forward credits a catch-up with a frame of audio that a host too slow to keep real time never actually delivers, and unbounded the estimate ran away until ordinary low readings tripped the "ahead of the card" arm and skipped frames on an almost empty queue. The chunk is the opened device's buffer — SdlAudio reads it back with SDL_GetAudioDeviceFormat() and logs Audio device buffer: N sample frames at F Hz -> pacing estimate envelope E ms — rounded up and never below ESTIMATE_ENVELOPE_MS (24 ms, SDL3's default 1024-frame buffer at 44.1 kHz). The header records the alternatives that were modelled and rejected, so read it before changing a threshold.

    When the host genuinely cannot keep real time, no pacing policy can conjure the missing samples, and two layers of last-level hold take over. The tick-side layer: SdlAudio pads the ring up to QUEUE_FLOOR_MS by repeating the last emitted level, because a DC hold has no discontinuity in it — a starved host stutters instead of clicking. Since that inserts samples the emulator never produced, it is armed only when pacing is in charge: not at a --speed other than 1x, and not during a tape fastload, where emulated time is deliberately decoupled from real time. But that pad only runs when a tick runs, so it cannot bridge a tick that never arrives — a GUI stall, a degraded timer, a host throttled below real time. The device-boundary layer covers exactly that: the SDL audio callback holds the last real pair for any shortfall (audio_fill.h), on SDL's audio thread, independent of tick cadence. Fill activity is reported on the periodic cadence: log line as audio-fill: N ms (M events) — on a healthy host it never appears.

    The ordering and lifetime around all of this live in src/platform/frame_sequencer.h, used by the Qt frontend and deliberately open-coded by the SDL one. It exists because the policy was well tested while the wiring was not: constructing a fresh pacing BandState every tick instead of persisting it passed the entire automated gate, and was caught only by watching a live cadence log.
