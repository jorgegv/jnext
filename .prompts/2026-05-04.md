#### Trace-evidence ranking (highest evidence first)

The G46(b) trace shows the firmware looping on writes to NR 0x56/0x57 (RAM bank-switch), NR 0x8E (paging trampoline), and NR 0xB8/0xB9/0xBA/0xBB (DivMMC RST trap entry-points — all already wired at emulator.cpp:1731-1736 per reviewer audit). The right way to attack is to re-derive the candidate list from what the firmware is **actually doing**, not from speculation about what it might be reading.

**Top — direct trace-driven audits (highest evidence, smallest blast radius):**

| Audit target | Source ref | Why first |
|--------------|-----------|-----------|
| `Mmu::set_page` for NR 0x56/0x57 (slot 6/7) RAM-test path | emulator.cpp:1281-1290; mmu.cpp/h `set_page` | Trace shows the loop body is exactly these two writes hammered repeatedly. Any off-by-one (à la the 2026-04-18 +0x20 shift bug) would manifest exactly like this. |
| `Mmu::write_nr_8e` for unified-paging trampoline | emulator.cpp:1800-1806; mmu `write_nr_8e` | Slot-2 trampoline writes 0x8E,0x03 then 0x8E,0x00 in the wrap path. If the wrap is non-VHDL-faithful, firmware loops. |
| NR 0x1E/0x1F active-line `frame_cycle_` anchor | emulator.cpp:1296-1305 | 2026-04-25 hypothesis (active-line timing skew) was never disproven. Computation is `(clock_.get() - frame_cycle_) / master_cycles_per_line`; mis-anchored `frame_cycle_` or wrong `master_cycles_per_line` would mis-report raster line during a polling loop. |
